            Dear Melina Dass:

            The Office of Prescription Drug Promotion (OPDP) of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration
            (FDA) has reviewed the promotional communication, an Instagram post (post) for JEUVEAU®
            (prabotulinumtoxinA-xvfs) for injection, for intramuscular use (Jeuveau) submitted by Evolus,
            Inc. (Evolus) under cover of Form FDA 2253.1 The post was made by Evolus on both the
            “evolus” and “jeuveaubyevolus” Instagram accounts. The FDA Bad Ad Program also received
            a complaint regarding this post for Jeuveau. FDA has determined that the post is false or
            misleading. Thus, the post misbrands Jeuveau within the meaning of the Federal Food, Drug,
            and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) and makes its distribution violative. 21 U.S.C. 352(a), (n);
            321(n); 331(a). 21 CFR 202.1(e)(5); (e)(7)(viii). These violations are concerning from a
            public health perspective because the promotional communication creates a misleading
            impression regarding the safety and effectiveness of Jeuveau, a drug with a number of
            serious and potentially life-threatening risks, including a boxed warning regarding the risk of
            distant spread of toxin effects.

            Prior Communication
            OPDP notes that our advisory comments dated November 22, 2019, to Evolus addressed
            draft promotional communications for Jeuveau with certain similarities to the claims and
            presentations included in the post addressed in this letter. In these advisory comments,
            OPDP recommended revisions to draft promotional communications in order to
            avoid misrepresenting the onset of treatment effect with Jeuveau. We are concerned that
            Evolus is not representing the benefits of Jeuveau in a truthful and non-misleading manner,
            despite concerns previously expressed by OPDP.

            Additionally, the reel is accompanied by the following in the caption (emphasis added):

                        “Lines today. Blurred in as little as 2 days.*…Nope. You’re not seeing things.
                        Jeuveau® really does work like that. Josephine is only one of the many patients who

            These claims and presentations are misleading for multiple reasons. First, the claims and
            presentations misleadingly suggest that treatment with Jeuveau will get “rid of” of frown lines,
            patients will experience “complete results,” and frown lines will be “gone” indefinitely, when
            this is not the case. According to the FDA-approved Prescribing Information (PI) for Jeuveau
            (emphasis added), “JEUVEAU is indicated for the temporary improvement in the
            appearance of moderate to severe glabellar lines associated with corrugator and/or procerus
            muscle activity in adult patients.” The efficacy of Jeuveau for this use was evaluated based
            on the proportion of subjects achieving ≥ 2-point improvement from baseline at maximum
            frown as assessed independently by both the investigator and subject as a composite
            endpoint using the Glabellar Line Scale (GLS) at Day 30. While the pivotal trials for Jeuveau
            included evaluations at specific GLS scores including a GLS score of 0 or “none”, these were
            exploratory endpoints, and no statistical analyses were performed. As such, no alpha was
            allocated to this analysis, meaning no pre-specified false positive error rate was established.
            Thus, claims suggesting that frown lines are “gone” or patients experienced “complete”
            results are not supported. We acknowledge that the full, FDA-approved indication for
            Jeuveau is included in the post, but not until the close of Josephine’s testimonial or unless
            the viewer manually scrolls down in the caption, neither of which mitigates the misleading
            impression created by the post.

            In addition to the misleading suggestion regarding the magnitude of efficacy, the post also
            creates a misleading impression regarding the onset of efficacy. Specifically, these claims
            and presentations suggest that Jeuveau provides faster results (by Day 2) than has been
            demonstrated. As mentioned above, the primary efficacy endpoint for Jeuveau was assessed

            at Day 30 in the pivotal studies (EV-001 and EV-002). While efficacy data were collected at
            other time points besides the primary endpoint at Day 30, the timepoint at Day 2 was an
            exploratory endpoint (i.e., hypothesis generating). Similar to the concerns addressed above,
            such analysis of this exploratory endpoint has limitations, such as the lack of multiplicity
            adjustment, which introduces statistical and inferential risks, making it difficult to interpret and
            draw reliable conclusions and does not ascertain whether the findings were attributable to
            treatment with Jeuveau, or merely due to chance. Furthermore, this exploratory endpoint at
            Day 2 assessed response as only a ≥1-point change on the GLS based on separate subject
            or investigator assessment. A one-grade change may not accurately represent a clinically
            meaningful intra-subject change because the response threshold for moving to an adjacent
            category on an assessment scale represents a much smaller level of improvement.

            In conjunction with claims and presentations regarding the primary efficacy endpoint, the
            inclusion of claims and presentations related to the exploratory endpoint at Day 2 conflates
            the measures of response and confounds consumers’ understanding of the efficacy of
            Jeuveau. These two endpoints do not reflect the same degree of clinical response. The
            primary endpoint (at Day 30) that was deemed clinically significant was a more stringent ≥ 2-
            point improvement on the GLS scale and was based on a composite of both investigator and
            subject assessments, whereas the exploratory endpoint (at Day 2) assessed only a ≥ 1-point
            improvement and only relied on a single assessment dimension. The intermingling of claims
            and presentations regarding these two distinct endpoints results in a misleading impression
            regarding the efficacy of Jeauveu as consumers are unlikely to differentiate between and
            understand the varying degrees of clinical response and statistical rigor represented
            throughout the presentation. Therefore, the totality of these claims and presentations in the
            post misleadingly suggests that treatment benefit with Jeuveau is more favorable, with
            respect to the magnitude and onset of efficacy, than has been demonstrated. While the
            patient testimonial in this video may be an accurate reflection of this patient’s own experience
            with Jeuveau, the testimonial does not adequately support the suggestion that all patients
            taking Jeuveau will experience a similar response. We acknowledge that the SUPER in the
            reel states “Individual results may vary.” However, this does not mitigate the misleading
            impression created by the post.

            The reel features Josephine, a Jeuveau patient, giving a lively and engaging patient
            testimonial/interview with trendy, upbeat music playing in the background. Josephine
            appears fashion-forward and beauty-focused in makeup, with aesthetic nails, wearing various
            pieces of jewelry, and pictured through various outfit changes all while touting the benefits
            and efficacy of Jeuveau. In addition, there are multiple embedded video clips of before and
            after treatment with Jeuveau at Day 0, Day 2, and Day 30. The reel is accompanied by the
            following claims in the caption of the post (emphasis added):

                        “…it’s no wonder Jeuveau is making moves to Studio @IPSY for our Blur The Lines
                        Masterclass. Because in 2026, we’re blurring the lines between what’s found in
                        your beauty bag and what happens in the treatment room. [syringe icon]
                        [lipstick icon]”

            The totality of these claims and presentations is misleading because it likens the use of an
            injectable prescription product with an everyday beauty product, oversimplifies the important
            dosage and administration instructions of Jeuveau and minimizes the risks associated with a

            prescription drug product with a boxed warning. Specifically, the claims and presentations
            imply that the use of Jeuveau, a prescription injectable product intended to be administered
            by a healthcare provider, is comparable to an everyday beauty product or regimen and can
            simply be ordered from a cosmetic delivery service such as IPSY and incorporated as a part
            of a cosmetic routine, similar to a stick of lipstick being stored in “your beauty bag.” However,
            Jeuveau is associated with important dosage and administration instructions for safe use.
            According to the DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION of the PI:

                        “The safe and effective use of Jeuveau depends upon proper storage of the product,
                        selection of the correct dose, and proper reconstitution and administration techniques.
                        Physicians administering JEUVEAU must understand the relevant neuromuscular
                        and/or orbital anatomy of the area involved…”

            The suggestion that lines are blurred between Jeuveau, a prescription product, and an over-
            the counter cosmetic is especially problematic from a public health perspective given the
            serious risks associated with the use of the product. The PI for Jeuveau includes a boxed
            warning for the distant spread of toxin effect, contraindications, and multiple warnings and
            precautions associated with the use of the product.

            This misleading suggestion is further compounded because, in contrast to the benefit
            presentations, the reel fails to present information related to the side effects and
            contraindications of Jeuveau with a prominence and readability reasonably comparable with
            the presentation of information relating to the benefits of Jeuveau. Specifically, risk
            information is presented only at the end of the reel, where it is unlikely to draw the viewer’s
            attention. It appears only after Josephine concludes her testimonial with the tag line,
            “Jeuveau, you see me” with the tag line subsequently appearing onscreen for about four
            seconds against a black background, which typically suggests the close of a presentation.
            However, a brief narrated statement of the indication and limited risk information does follow
            with additional unnarrated, fast scrolling risk information appearing after a directive to “ask
            your doctor if Jeuveau is right for you.” The reel does not present any signal, at anytime
            throughout, to alert the viewer that important risk information is presented after the close of
            Josephine’s 38 second testimonial. We acknowledge the prescription status and limited risk
            information for Jeuveau is disclosed in the lower half of the caption of the post; however, it
            can only be accessed if the viewer manually scrolls down past additional benefit claims. The
            overall effect of disclosing risk information in this manner, compared to the presentation
            Jeuveau’s benefits during the patient testimonial, undermines the communication of risk
            information and thereby minimizes the risks associated with the use of Jeuveau.

            Conclusion and Requested Action

            For the reasons described above, the post misbrands Jeuveau within the meaning of the
            FD&C Act and makes its distribution violative. 21 U.S.C. 352(a), (n); 321(n); 331(a). 21 CFR
            202.1(e)(5); (e)(7)(viii).
